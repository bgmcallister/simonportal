In the sweltering dust of nineteen sixty-five Palm Springs, Harold McAllister, a man of quiet labor and unshakeable dignity, presents his fifteen-year-old son, Gregory, with the ultimate symbol of freedom: a nineteen sixty Chevy K-ten pickup. But in a society fractured by systemic hurdles and racial prejudice, freedom is never free for a Black family in the American West.

As the desert heat ripples over the asphalt, the lives of the McAllister and Blue families become intertwined through friendship, hardship, injustice, and the bonds that hold people together when everything around them seems determined to pull them apart. An act of injustice ripples through both families, leaving wounds that will reach far beyond the circumstances that created them.

And somewhere along the way, Roy and Jerry Greene enter the story.
－Two white men whose decisions would have profound impact on both families.
From the sun-baked streets of nineteen-sixties Palm Springs to a nineteen ninety-one Oakland coffee shop, Greg “Big G-Mack” McAllister begins piecing together fragments of history, and a missing bloodline.

What emerges is a story of family, friendship, race, identity, loss, survival, and the strange paths that connect one generation to another.

Because sometimes the people who change your life aren't the ones standing in the center of the story.
