Chapter 9: The Name She Never Knew
The coffee shop wasn't anything special. A little place tucked between old brick buildings, the kind of spot where the same folks sat in the same chairs every morning, reading the same newspapers and arguing about the same things they had been arguing about for twenty years. The kind of place that didn't care who you were when you walked in. It just gave you a cup of coffee and a quiet corner. And that's exactly what I needed. Because some conversations ain't meant to happen standing up. Some conversations need a table between two people. A little time. A little room for the world to stop spinning. I sat there waiting, hands wrapped around a coffee cup that had gone mostly untouched. Outside the window, Oakland moved along like it always did. Cars passing. People walking. Life happening. Funny thing about life... The world can keep moving even when yours feels like it stopped. Then the bell above the door rang. And there she was. Loren. I watched her look around until she spotted me. She gave a small smile and walked over. She still had that same curious look in her eyes. That same way Bubba used to look when he was trying to figure something out. Like the world was a puzzle, and he was just waiting for somebody to hand him the missing pieces. She sat down across from me. "Hey, Greg." "Hey, kiddo." For a second, neither one of us said anything. Because what do you say when you're about to change somebody's entire understanding of where they came from? I took a slow breath. "Loren... before I say anything, I want you to know something." She leaned forward slightly. "Okay..." "I didn't come here to bring you some sad story. I didn't come here to reopen some old wound. I'm here because I believe some things belong to people... even if it takes twenty-five years to finally find their way home." She looked confused, but she listened. And that was something Bubba would've appreciated. That boy always said the most important thing a person could do was listen. "Back in 1966," I said, "your mother met somebody." Her expression changed. Not fear. Not yet. Just curiosity. "Somebody who changed my life." I looked down at my coffee for a second. "His name was Benjamin Buford Blue." Her eyes narrowed slightly. "Benjamin...?" I nodded. "Yeah. But nobody called him that." A tiny smile crossed my face. "Everybody called him Bubba." The name hung there between us. And then I saw it. A little flicker. Like something in her already knew. "My mom knew him?" "Yeah, she did." I leaned back. "Your mom was the first person who ever called him Bubba." Loren froze. "Wait... what?" I laughed quietly. "Yeah. That name everybody knew him by? That came from her." I told her about that summer day. The park. The fountain. A young girl named Susie Anderson. A nervous teenage boy who didn't know what to say. A stupid joke from his best friend that accidentally created a name that would follow him the rest of his life. And Loren just sat there, taking it all in. "My mom gave him that name..." "She sure did." I smiled. "And the funny thing is... from the moment Bubba saw her, he was gone." She laughed softly. "Gone?" "Oh yeah. Completely." I shook my head. "That boy could talk for three hours about shrimp boats, engines, fishing, or some crazy idea he had... but when Susie walked into the room? Suddenly Benjamin Buford Blue forgot how words worked." That got a real laugh out of her. A little piece of Bubba coming through. Then her smile faded. "Greg..." "Yeah?" "I always wondered." Her voice got quieter. "I wondered who my dad was." There it was. The question that had probably lived in her heart her whole life. Not anger. Not resentment. Just a missing piece. "I didn't know if he left. I didn't know if he didn't want me. I just... I never knew." I nodded slowly. "Well, kiddo..." I reached into my jacket pocket. "I think it's time you meet him." I pulled out an old photograph. The edges were worn. The kind of wear that doesn't come from being forgotten. It comes from being held. She took it carefully. And when she looked down... She stopped. The picture was me and Bubba as teenagers. Two dumb kids standing beside an old car, both of us grinning like we owned the world. Loren stared. "That's him?" "That's him." She looked closer. "Wow..." "What?" She looked up. "He looks like me." I smiled. "Yeah." She looked back at the picture. "The eyes." "Yep." "The smile." "Yep." She shook her head. "That's crazy." I leaned back. "No, kiddo. That's family." And then I told her about him. Not just the big things. Not just the war. Not just the tragedy. I told her about the real Bubba. The kid who could make anybody laugh. The guy who would give you the shirt off his back and apologize because he didn't have a second shirt to give you. The man who believed everybody deserved respect. The man who loved her mother. The man who never knew he had a daughter. And that was the part that hurt the most. "He would've loved you." She looked down. "You think so?" I didn't hesitate. "I know so." The coffee shop got quiet. Not empty. Just quiet. The kind of quiet where something important has been said. After a while, I leaned forward. "Loren..." She looked up. "I want you to understand something. You lost a father you never got to meet. I lost a brother I never got to say goodbye to." I paused. "But neither one of us has to carry that alone anymore." Her eyes got a little wet. I smiled. "So here's the deal. You got a whole lifetime of questions, and I got a whole lifetime of stories." She laughed softly. "That sounds like a lot of stories." I grinned. "Kid, you have no idea." I tapped the table. "So whenever you need somebody to talk to... whenever you want to hear about your dad... you got me." I looked her straight in the eye. "And if you're okay with it... you can consider me family." A small smile crossed her face. "Family?" "Yeah." I shrugged. "You can just call me Uncle G." She looked down, smiling. Then back at me. "I'd like that." She took a breath. "Uncle G." That one got me. After twenty-five years of carrying a memory, hearing that name felt like something settling back into place. Then she smiled. "But if I'm family..." She tilted her head. "You can't keep calling me Loren like I'm some stranger." I laughed. "Alright then. What do I call you?" She smiled. "Roxy." I nodded. "Roxy it is." And just like that... The past found a new name. The drive back to Palm Springs was quieter than the drive up. Not sad quiet. Different. The kind of quiet where your mind is sorting through a thousand thoughts at once. I kept thinking about Bubba. About how impossible it was that after all these years, some part of him was still out there. Not just a memory. Not just a story. A person. His daughter. Roxy. About a week after I got home, I walked out to the mailbox and found a letter waiting. No fancy envelope. No special markings. Just my name written across the front. I knew who it was before I opened it. I took it inside. Sat down at the kitchen table. And read. --- Uncle G, I don't really know where to start. I've been thinking about our conversation every day since you left. I keep looking at that picture you gave me. I keep thinking about my father. Benjamin. Bubba. It's strange. I spent my whole life wondering who he was, and now I feel like I know him better than I ever thought possible. But I also realize how much I don't know. I want to hear the stories. The little things. The things nobody writes down. The things that made him him. I've decided to take some time away from the record store. I think I need a vacation. I think I need some time to understand all this. I was thinking maybe I could come out to Palm Springs for a week or so. I figured I'd get a hotel room nearby and we could spend some time talking. If that's okay. I left my number below. I hope to hear from you soon. —Roxy --- I read it twice. Then a third time. I folded it carefully. Sat there for a minute. Then I smiled. Because sometimes life gives you something back. Not exactly what you lost. But something you didn't know you were missing. I picked up the phone. --- The next morning, I called her. She answered after a few rings. "Hello?" "Hey, Roxy." A pause. Then a smile in her voice. "Uncle G?" "That's me." "I was hoping you'd call." "Well, I got your letter." "I wasn't sure if—" "Roxy." "Yeah?" I chuckled. "First thing's first. You ain't staying in some roach-infested hotel while you're out here." She laughed. "What?" "I said what I said." "Greg—" "Uncle G." She laughed again. "Okay. Uncle G." "I got a spare room. It's clean. It's got a bed. The walls ain't falling down. Usually." "Usually?" "Hey, I didn't say the place was fancy." She laughed. And man... I hadn't heard that sound from somebody connected to Bubba in twenty-five years. "Besides," I continued, "it'd be nice having somebody around for a little while." "Yeah?" "Yeah. Somebody to listen to an old man ramble about the past." She smiled. "I'd like that." "And maybe hold a flashlight for me in the garage once in a while." She laughed. "Is that part of the deal?" "Absolutely." I looked out the window toward the garage. Toward the old truck sitting inside. "And there's somebody else I think you oughta meet." A pause. "Who?" I smiled. "Emerald Thunder." Silence. Then: "The truck?" "Yeah." "The one my dad knew?" "That's the one." I could hear the excitement in her voice. "I can't wait." I leaned back in my chair. "Well, Roxy..." I looked out toward the desert sun. "She's been waiting a long time to meet you." And somewhere deep inside that old garage... I swear that old Chevy sounded just a little bit happier.

Chapter 10: The Daughter of Bubba Blue
Roxy came to Palm Springs a few weeks later. Funny thing about life... You spend twenty-five years believing a door is closed. Then one day, somebody knocks. And when you open it? You realize it was never locked. It was just waiting for the right person. I saw her pull into the driveway just after noon. A little rental car rolling through the desert dust, sunlight flashing across the hood. I was standing beside Emerald Thunder, one hand resting on that old green paint. For the first time in years, I wasn't looking at that truck and seeing what I lost. I was looking at what survived. When Roxy stepped out of the car, she stopped. She didn't say anything at first. She just stared. And I understood. Some things don't need an introduction. "That's her," I said. Her eyes stayed locked on the truck. "Emerald Thunder." I nodded. "Yeah." She walked closer, slow and careful. Like she was walking up to something she had only ever known through stories. And maybe she was. Because that truck carried more memories than most people ever get the chance to make. She reached out and ran her fingers along the old scar in the door. Bubba's name. Still there. She smiled faintly. "He did this?" I nodded. "Yeah." She looked back at me. "Why?" I laughed softly. "Well... that's a story." And standing there in that garage, with Bubba's daughter beside me, I told her about the summer of 1967. About two dumb kids who thought they knew everything. About Bubba carving his name into that door before leaving for Vietnam. About how angry I was when I first saw it. How I thought he had ruined something special. Then I told her what he told me. It wasn't damage. It was a promise. A piece of himself he was leaving behind. Something that said he had been there. That our friendship mattered. That some things were worth carrying with you. She listened the same way Bubba used to. Quiet. Focused. Like every word deserved to be remembered. And that was the first thing I noticed. She didn't just look like him. She had pieces of him. The way she listened. The way she thought before she spoke. The way she cared about the little details. For a moment, it almost felt like I was standing there with my old friend again. Only this time... he had sent his daughter. --- Over the next few days, I showed Roxy Palm Springs. Not the tourist version. Not the postcards. I showed her our version. The places where two dumb teenagers became brothers. Lake Cahuilla. The old diner where our mothers used to work. The roads where Bubba and I used to cruise until the sun disappeared behind the mountains. Everywhere we went, there was another memory. And Roxy wanted every one of them. She asked questions I never expected anybody to ask. "What was he like when he was angry?" "What made him laugh?" "Was he scared?" That last one always hit different. Because the answer wasn't easy. I looked down for a second. "Yeah, kiddo." She looked at me. "He was." I nodded. "But that's the thing about Bubba. He was scared and he went anyway." I took a breath. "That's what made him who he was." And that was the part I wanted her to know. Not just the stories. Not just the legend. The man. The real person underneath it all. --- One afternoon, after another long talk in the garage, Roxy asked me something. "Uncle G?" "Yeah?" "Do you think there are still things we don't know about him?" I looked over at Emerald Thunder. Funny thing about that truck... She always seemed to have another story hiding somewhere. "After all these years?" I smiled. "With Bubba?" I shook my head. "That man was full of surprises." I didn't know then how right I was. --- A couple days later, I decided to take Roxy for a drive. Just me, her, and Emerald Thunder. No big plans. No destination. Just letting the old girl roll. Sometimes the road knows where you're supposed to end up better than you do. We drove through town, past places that had changed and places that somehow stayed frozen in time. Eventually, I found myself slowing down. Because I knew the place. An old dealership. Or what was left of it. The building was still standing, but barely. Faded signs hung crooked above broken windows. The parking lot was cracked and overgrown. The kind of place that looked like time had forgotten it. Roxy looked over. "What is this place?" I stared at the building. "Funny thing..." I put the truck in park. "This is where Emerald Thunder started." She looked at me. "The dealership?" I nodded. "Yep." I looked through the windshield at the old building. "Back in 1960, a man named Roy Greene drove this truck right out those doors brand new." I smiled faintly. "The father of a man I knew named Jerry" Roxy looked over. "Oh yeah? Roy is Jerry's dad? Same Jerry with the Gremlin, right?" I replied, "Yeah, same Jerry. He was the one who bought Thunder from me." I looked back at the old building. "His father was the first man who owned her."  --- We sat there for a moment. Then we heard a voice. "Hey there..." We both turned. An old man stood near the entrance of the building. "I know that truck." I felt something shift. The kind of feeling you get when the past suddenly realizes you're looking for it. The old man walked closer, his eyes fixed on Emerald Thunder. "That ain't just some old Chevy." I stepped out. "You know her?" He smiled. "Son..." He looked over the truck carefully. "I knew her before she was green." --- The old man explained that the dealership had been closed for years, but he had helped clean it out when ownership changed hands. A lot of things were left behind. Old advertisements. Customer records. Photographs. Paperwork nobody had bothered to throw away. "There's still some stuff in the back office," he said. "Nobody's touched it in years." We followed him inside. The building creaked around us. Dust covered everything. But underneath all of that... there were pieces of history still sitting exactly where people had left them. Old showroom photos. Sales records. Stacks of paperwork. A whole world that had been forgotten. Then the old man stopped. He pulled a folder from a cabinet. "I remember this one because I thought it was strange." He opened it. Inside was a copy of the original bill of sale. I leaned closer. Roxy stepped beside me. And together, we read it. The truck. The year. The dealership. The original buyer. Bernard Blue. I stared at the name. For a second, the room went quiet. Then I looked at Roxy. She wasn't looking at the truck anymore. She wasn't looking at the dealership. She was looking at that name. Because she knew it. "Bernard..." she whispered. I looked at her. "Yeah, kiddo." Her fingers touched the paper. "My grandfather." And right there, standing in an old dealership surrounded by forgotten pieces of yesterday, the past finally spoke clearly. This wasn't just about Emerald Thunder. This wasn't just about who owned an old Chevy pickup. This was about a man whose story had been buried. A man named Bernard Blue. Roxy's grandfather. Bubba's father. The man who brought home a brand-new blue pickup truck before his son ever touched the steering wheel. --- That night, back at the house, Roxy and I sat in the garage. The paperwork rested on the workbench. Emerald Thunder sat quietly behind us. Like she had been waiting all these years for somebody to finally ask the right questions. "I always thought I knew the whole story," I said. Roxy looked at me. "But you didn't." I shook my head. "No." I picked up the copy of the bill of sale. "Apparently, neither did Jerry." I looked at the name again. Bernard Blue. A name that had been buried for decades. A name that belonged to a family history nobody had finished telling. Jerry Greene. The man who bought Thunder from me. The man who had his own piece of the story. The man who said his dad Roy was the original owner. The only person who could have the answers we need. I looked out toward the desert night. "There's one person I need to talk to." Roxy waited. "Who?" I folded the paper. A lot of years had passed. A lot of miles had gone by. But some conversations... they don't disappear. They just wait. "Jerry Greene." And for the first time in a long time... I got that familiar feeling again... The feeling of a past, sitting out there somewhere, waiting to be found.

Chapter 11: The Sacred Relay
Sleep never found me that night. Not even for a minute. I stayed out in the garage until dawn, sitting in my old lawn chair with Emerald Thunder parked a few feet away. The desert air was warm and still, carrying that familiar scent of sagebrush and baked asphalt. Every now and then, I’d catch myself staring at Thunder, my mind drifting back to that nineteen-sixty bill of sale. It's crazy, how one piece of paper can make a man question thirty years of memories. Some questions don’t leave you alone. They settle down somewhere deep inside, like a slow leak in a tire. You can drive on it for a while... but sooner or later, you’ve gotta pull over and deal with it. By the time morning finally rolled around, I knew there was only one person left who could fill in the missing pieces, but I didn't know if he was still around. It had been over twenty years, and time doesn't wait for anybody.. I dug through my old Rolodex until I found the number I was looking for. Jerry Greene. Turns out he’d been living at an assisted living facility over in Palm Desert for a while now. Time had a way of catching up with all of us eventually. Even the people we remembered as larger than life. I picked up the phone and gave him a call. "Jerry? It’s Greg... Greg McAllister." There was a pause on the other end.
