The Legacy of Benny Blue: The Life and Times of Big G-Mack McAllister
Chapter 1: Desert Roots and the Deep Green Gift Everyone knows "Bubba" by now... The shrimp-loving friend of Forrest Gump, who died on the edge of a river in Vietnam... Well... Long before the war? ... Before he fell in love with shrimp and seafood? ... Before he met that skinny kid named Gump at basic training? ... There was Benny Blue, and the Coachella Valley of California... Hey folks, let me introduce myself. The name's Gregory DeShawn McAllister, but out here? Everybody just know me as Big G-Mack. Born in 1950. Palm Springs, California... Where the valley was an oven, and the streets smelled of hot asphalt and thirsty ambition. Pops, Harold, put his soul into his family. He'd lay bricks all day in Coachella Valley, his hands rough as desert stone, mama Ernestine held the house down like a queen guarding her throne. Life back then was a little different for us black, working class families－ especially in a place like Palm Springs and the Coachella Valley. When most people think of Palm Springs? They think of the classy life. Big swimming pools, VIP dining. Expensive shopping. Maybe catching a glimpse of a celebrity on vacation... North of the city though? You had the gears of the civilization－ A community all on its own, full of working class families...North Palm Springs and Desert Hot Springs... To me? That's home. Back then? Life was golden man...Hot summers, Schwinn bikes with chains that smelled of grease and adventure... radios bumpin' The Temptations, and a sky so wide you could almost lose yourself in it. Childhood was amazing, man.－Mostly 'cause of my best friend Benjamin. Benny was four months older, born in '49. My mentor, my big brother in everything but blood. His mama, Miss Loretta, ran a single-mom empire raisin' six kids, yet somehow let me slip under her wing too. Family dinners? Skillet fryin catfish we'd pull from Lake Cahuilla every week. The smell of hot fish and butter, thick in the air like it owned the room. Benny and I would fish, bring it home, and the whole house licked their lips. Ain't nothing beat that sizzle, that first bite that made your knees weak. Me and Benny dreamed of startin' a seafood spot. We fell in love with fishing while taking our frequent ride-along trips with Benny's uncle Felix on his shrimping boat. Prices on lobster and shrimp kept us waitin', but man, we believed. See, every one of those big wins traces back to little sparks, to a time before I knew how much life would ask of me. I was fifteen when Dad handed me the keys to Emerald Thunder, a deep green 1960 Chevy k-10 pickup. I'll never forget the first time I saw her gleaming in the afternoon haze, as I turned to pops and gave him the biggest hug I had ever given in life. He'd worked night shifts for weeks to save every penny. That truck—my truck—finally mine... Well... I should really say, "ours". See, dad didn't want me driving until I got my license, but Bubba and I— fifteen, big dreams..We already made big plans for her within a couple hours. That night,though, Dad went into work. Fate had other plans. He never came home. Car accident stole him the day he gave me that truck, leaving Emerald Thunder a vessel of memories, lessons, and love I could never repay. Benny took it hard, too. He looked up to my dad, he practically modeled himself after him. See, his old man wasn't in the picture anymore. We didn't really talk about it much, but I knew it was on his mind at times... My dad was friends with him when we were young, too. That's how Benny and I met. Back then, our dads worked at the San Diego docks, riding together every work day, and laying brick together on the weekends. When pops started laying brick full time,he hired a few other guys, while Benny's dad started fixing cars on the weekends. I guess they just naturally fell out of touch for awhile. Our moms, trying to keep us in line, while waitressing together at the diner, forged a friendship that also echoed into our lives. Ever since, Emerald Thunder has always been considered Benny's truck just as much as it was mine. ### Chapter 2: July Sparks and the Birth of a Moniker I think back to when Benny and I were just two kids figuring out respect, pride, and loyalty in a garage that hummed with possibility. One day in particular... It was July 4th of 1966. Sun blazing was over the valley like a broiler, and the garage buzzing like it had its own heartbeat. Benny and I got into it early that day, not over engines this time, but him overstepping, acting like he was some kind of dad. Kept saying I was fallin' in with the wrong crew... That I needed to watch myself, that he wasn't gonna let me screw up. I shot back, trying to make him see I could handle my business. Voices got louder, hands waving over greasy parts, but then something shifted—we ran out of words, the heat of the day and weight of our words hangin' between us. Benny leaned against the workbench, arms crossed, jaw tight. I leaned on my old Chevy Biscayne, staring at the chrome glinting in the sun, feeling the tension coil. Silence stretched, heavy and humming. Eventually, I started talkin' softer, explaining my side, bridging the gap without letting pride strangle it. Benny didn't say much, just listened, edge softened. Not resolved fully, but silence broken—that was enough. We finally moved past it and decided to take the Biscayne for a spin. Rolling down cracked streets, Chevy gleamed in the sun, chrome bumpers catching rays like little mirrors. “Man, look at that classic slick,” I said, nodding at the smooth lines and low rumble. Benny smiled, “Yeah... she's got personality.” That's how we talked about cars—personality, not just metal. As we cruised near the park where kids were setting off fireworks, Benny spotted a group of teens by the fountain. One girl caught his eye—Susie Anderson... She had short hair, a confident grin, laughing at something her friend said. Benny froze, awkward for once... See, Benny had been talking about Susie Anderson for at least a month now... But too afraid to ask her out. I had an idea. I was gonna make sure Susie noticed Benny. Quick thinking, I pulled the Biscayne over, and got out of the drivers seat, acting like it was Benny's car. “Yeah man, she drives real nice. You've got a great car here.” I said loudly, so the girl would overhear, giving him a nudge. She turned to look at us, and asked, “Whose Chevy is that?” Benny was caught flat-footed. Before he could stumble, I hollered back to her ”It's my brother's car!" She tilted her head, confused. She thought I had said "Bubba's car"... She replied, “Who's Bubba!?” Benny, being flustered in the moment, stammered, “Oh, that's me.” I laughed like hell. And just like that, the name stuck. From that night on, Benny wasn't just Benny—he was Bubba. I let him drive the girl home in the Biscayne while I walked back to my house, fireworks painting the sky. I watched the Biscayne's taillights fade down Palm Canyon Drive, those red dots shrinking into the night like fireflies slipping through your fingers. The air carried that desert perfume — a mixture of spent fireworks, gasoline, and heat that clings to your skin long after you leave it behind. I stuffed my hands in my pockets, letting the warmth of the evening settle over me. I passed the newly-built Tramway Gas Station, its fluorescent lights buzzing faintly like they were still learning to wake up. Old man Reyes inside leaned against the counter, flipping a newspaper lazily, glancing up only long enough to give me a nod. The smell of oil and fresh asphalt hung around the pumps, and I thought how strange it was that something so new could feel like it'd been part of the neighborhood forever. North of the station, the desert stretched wide, the wind turbines on the hills north of the city catching the last light of the sun, each blade throwing back tiny, fleeting sparks of gold and silver, flickering like distant fireflies dancing on invisible strings. I turned east onto the dirt road that led straight to my house, my footfalls kicking up little clouds of sand, sparkling faintly in the fading glow. Off to the side, a wide scar of earth marked where the new highway was being carved — folks around town whispered about it, calling it “the future road,” the one that would eventually be known as the I-10 Interstate. It wasn't like the streets we grew up on; there were no curbs, no familiar names, just the promise of a path that hadn't fully arrived yet. By the time I reached the edge of my block, the last echoes of fireworks had faded, replaced by the soft hum of evening cicadas and the occasional distant engine echoing through the canyon. That night carried a kind of weight, a feeling that something was about to change, though I couldn't have named it yet. Somewhere between the flickering shadows of the wind turbines and the glow still lingering over the peaks, I felt the pull of home, the familiar hum of the yard, and memories of the million times I've heard Dad hollar at me to get in the house. The "future road" eventually arrived, and with it, the spring of 1967. ### Chapter 3: The Future Road and the Marking of Steel Morning hung soft over the Coachella Valley, a pale haze stretched between the mountains as if the desert itself hadn't quite woken. Emerald Thunder, my treasured 1960 Chevy pickup truck sat in my garage. Bubba and I just got back from having breakfast at a diner where our mothers once worked double shifts. The sun had barely crested the horizon, spilling a honeyed glow across the desert streets, when Bubba and I started working on Emerald Thunder a bit. I had some new exhaust I wanted to install before Bubba left town. Bubba had a ticket folded in his shirt pocket — a one-way bus ride, leaving out of San Bernardino, carrying him toward a new life in uniform. The war overseas had finally reached his doorstep, and though he'd taken the news with that calm grin of his, I could feel the weight between us. ...We were elbow-deep in the Chevy, tinkering and laughing, and Bubba, with that mischievous grin of his, wandered away from the engine bay... "Ay Benny you see where that hose clamp went?" I glanced to the passenger side of the truck and saw him kneeling down. Hearing a faint scraping noise, I walked around to where Bubba was, to see him carving into Thunder's door panel... “WHAT?... WHAT IN THE HELL ARE YOU DOIN', YOU IDIOT!?!?!” I shouted, half in anger, half in disbelief. Bubba, startled and holding his hands up, ready to explain himself. “G-Mack, hold up brother, hold up! I just wanted to leave my mark on Thunder. You know G... just... in case.” I shook my head. “Aw Damn, Bubba...Awww, man... you know how important this truck is to me... what are you thinkin', man?“ ...I looked at the carved-up door panel... ran my hand down the sharp edges of the scarred steel...Then, I did something I rarely did back in those days. I paused, and just thought for a second... and that's all it took. Just a second... I took my hand off the scratched door panel, with a slight grin on my face... I turned to Bubba, and I said what just... Felt right... “Hey, you know what, Bubba?” I said. He looked at me, a little uneasy... “Yeah, G-Mack?” I pointed at the door panel with a big smile. “Go ahead and finish it, buddy.” ...Bubba's grin widened as he traced the last line of his name into the green paint of my truck, Emerald Thunder. The rasp of the blade against metal sang in the garage like a hymn. I ran a hand down my face, shaking my head at my own crazy change of heart... But we were both smiling... We took a second just to look at each other... Just sitting there smiling like a couple geeks... But we took it all in. We said more words in our smiles than we ever said to anyone in either of our lifetimes... We both reached out and hugged each other with the combined strength that would rival a bear hug from pops. Haha, You lucky as hell my pops aint here to see you scratching up a chevy door like that.” “Shoot, G... Pops gave it to you, right? That makes it yours. And you? You're my brother. I just wanted to leave a piece of me here before I go, you know? ...Ay, and if I learned one thing about yo' daddy? that man loved seein' brotherhood. And this mark I made honors that... And besides, you think I'm gonna travel half way around the world and let Emerald Thunder forget about me?...” I smiled, as he continued. “Hey, you know, Big G, come to think of it? for all those reasons, I think this here etching would be... “pops-approved“! Ha!” For a heartbeat, the smell of exhaust and warm desert air wrapped around us, filling the garage with quiet. I couldn't help but smile. That boy always knew how to turn something into positivity. I didn't think much of it then, but, looking back... He was right. Pops was a strict man. Lived by the rules. But with that being said, the man prioritized relationships. The man wore his heart on his sleeve, and always emphasized kinship and the need for compassion in this world. Being the only real stand-up black man in his life, Bubba clung to my daddy's word like gospel. Pops lived the way a man should live. It didn't matter the circumstances, he always treated his neighbor with kindness and respect. In a time where skin color mattered a lot more than it does now, Pops didn't give a shit what shade you were, or what beef you may have had with him. He presented himself the same, and treated everyone the same. Despite the struggle in a stereotyping society, when pops went, Bubba took it hard. After all, he modeled himself after that man. He respected the hell out of him. Every once in awhile, at some random moment, I'll glance over at Bubba... Maybe tying a knot in his fishing line, or crankin' on a hard-to-reach bolt... and I'll see Pops in him. "Hey yo, G-Mack!", Bubba hollered at me from across the garage. "Man, look at these beautiful truck" I let out a breath, gave Bubba a light shove. “Yeah man, for a shit box Chevy...
