Emerald Thunder Chain of Title
From the Forrest Gump Universe, The Legacy of Bubba Blue: The Life and Times of Big G-Mack McAllister

This document provides a concise ownership history of Emerald Thunder. Its purpose is to help future adaptations accurately track the truck's journey through the story without confusing chronology or creating continuity errors [1]. The original novella remains the primary source [1].



IMPORTANT ADAPTATION RULE
This document is a continuity reference only [1]. It contains the complete ownership history of Emerald Thunder, including historical information that is intentionally hidden from Greg and the audience until later chapters [2]. Future adaptations should use this document to maintain factual accuracy, but they must preserve the timing of every revelation [2]. The audience should only learn hidden ownership information when Greg discovers it [2]. Never reveal ownership history, hidden identities, or historical connections before their designated discovery scenes [2]. This document explains what is historically true, not when the audience should learn it [3].



REVELATION MAP
This section identifies when major historical truths become available within the story. It exists to prevent accidental spoilers during adaptations [3].

Chapter 10 — Discovery of the Bill of Sale
The following revelations occur:
   Bernard Blue is revealed as the original owner of Emerald Thunder [3].
   The truck's original Baby Blue color is confirmed [3].
   Greg learns the truck's connection to the Blue family is much deeper than he understood [3].

Chapter 11 — Jerry Greene's Final Explanation
The following revelations occur:
   The identity of the "Desert Guardian Angel" is revealed as Roy Greene [4].
   Roy Greene's connection to Bernard Blue is explained [4].
   The Sacred Relay and the full purpose behind the truck's journey are revealed [4].



1960
Original Owner
(Hidden Until Chapter 10 — Discovery of the Bill of Sale)
   Owner: Bernard Blue [4].
   Location: Palm Springs, California [4].
   Historical Truth: Bernard Blue purchases a brand-new 1960 Chevrolet K-10 from a Palm Springs dealership [4, 5]. The truck leaves the dealership painted Baby Blue [5].
   Greg's Knowledge: Unknown [5].
   Audience Knowledge: Unknown until Chapter 10 [5].



Early 1960s
Protection and Concealment
(Hidden Until Chapter 11 — Jerry Greene's Final Explanation)
   Custodian: Roy Greene [5].
   Historical Truth: After Bernard Blue is wrongfully imprisoned, Roy Greene takes possession of Emerald Thunder to prevent it from being seized, lost, or forgotten [5]. To conceal the truck's identity, Roy repaints it Emerald Green [5, 6]. He quietly protects the truck for years while honoring a promise made to Bernard [6].
   Greg's Knowledge: Unknown [6].
   Audience Knowledge: Unknown until Chapter 11 [6].



1965
Harold McAllister
   Owner: Harold McAllister [6].
   Historical Truth: Before Bernard Blue's imprisonment, Harold McAllister visits Bernard and Roy Greene's shop while searching for a truck for his fifteen-year-old son Greg's birthday [6]. Bernard knows Harold is looking for the right vehicle and remembers his interest [7]. After Bernard is wrongfully imprisoned and Jerry Greene becomes connected with Emerald Thunder, Jerry remembers Harold's earlier visit and knows the truck would be going to a good family [7]. Emerald Thunder eventually comes into Harold McAllister's possession [7]. Harold later gives the truck to his fifteen-year-old son, Greg, as a surprise gift [7].
   Greg's Understanding: Greg believes his father simply worked hard, saved his money, and found the perfect used truck for his birthday [7]. He has no knowledge of Bernard Blue, Roy Greene, or the truck's hidden history [8].
   Audience Knowledge: At this point, the audience should experience the same understanding as Greg [8]. The deeper connection between Emerald Thunder and the Blue and Greene families remains hidden [8].



1965–1967
Gregory "Big G-Mack" McAllister
   Owner: Gregory McAllister [8].
   Historical Truth: Greg receives Emerald Thunder from his father [8]. The truck becomes his strongest connection to Harold after Harold dies later that same day [8]. Over the next two years, Emerald Thunder becomes inseparable from Greg's friendship with Bubba [8, 9].
   Audience Knowledge: Fully known [9].



Summer 1967
Jerry Greene
   Owner: Jerry Greene [9].
   Historical Truth: Greg sells Emerald Thunder to Jerry Greene [9]. Jerry intentionally pays more than Greg expects because he understands the value and history of the truck [9]. During their conversation, Greg learns portions of the truck's history, but not the complete truth [9].
   Audience Knowledge: The audience should only know what Greg learns during this conversation [10].



Late 1967
Roy Greene
(Roy's possession and identity remain hidden until Chapter 11.)
   Custodian: Roy Greene [10].
   Historical Truth: After Greg sells Emerald Thunder to Jerry Greene, the truck later comes into Roy Greene's possession [10]. Following Greg's automobile accident and Bubba's death, Roy quietly gives Greg the opportunity to buy the truck back [10].
   Greg's Knowledge: Greg believes this extraordinary reunion is little more than coincidence [11]. The true reason behind Roy's involvement is not revealed until Jerry explains the Sacred Relay decades later [11]. Greg knows only that a kind stranger had his old truck [11].
   Audience Knowledge: The audience should not yet know that the stranger is Roy Greene [11].



Late 1967 Forward
Gregory "Big G-Mack" McAllister
   Long-Term Caretaker: Gregory McAllister [11].
   Historical Truth: Greg once again becomes Emerald Thunder's caretaker [12]. Over the following decades, the truck remains with him as both a reminder of his father and a lasting connection to Bubba [12]. By 1991, Greg discovers that Emerald Thunder represents far more than his own memories [12].
   Audience Knowledge: The deeper meaning unfolds gradually throughout the remainder of the story [12].



Complete Historical Ownership Timeline
This timeline represents the actual historical ownership of Emerald Thunder [12]. It is provided for continuity purposes only and is not the order in which the audience should learn these facts [13].

1.  1960: Bernard Blue purchases Emerald Thunder (Originally painted Baby Blue) [13].
2.  Early 1960s: Roy Greene protects the truck and repaints it Emerald Green [13].
3.  1965: Harold McAllister acquires Emerald Thunder after Jerry Greene remembers Harold's earlier search for a truck [13].
4.  1965–1967: Gregory McAllister owns Emerald Thunder [13].
5.  Summer 1967: Jerry Greene purchases Emerald Thunder [13].
6.  Late 1967: Roy Greene quietly regains possession [13, 14].
7.  Late 1967–Present: Gregory McAllister becomes Emerald Thunder's lifelong caretaker [14].



Final Adaptation Rule
Emerald Thunder must never be treated as simply a vehicle [14]. Across the story it becomes:
   A father's gift [14].
   A symbol of brotherhood [14].
   A reminder of sacrifice [14].
   A vessel of memories [14].
   A connection between families [14].
   A promise carried across generations [14].
   A living record of love, loyalty, and legacy [14].

Maintain two separate truths at all times:
   Historical Truth: Who actually owned the truck throughout its history [15].
   Narrative Truth: When Greg—and therefore the audience—discovers each piece of that history [15].

Historical accuracy should never come at the expense of the story's intended mystery, emotional pacing, or revelations [15].
