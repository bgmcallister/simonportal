Chapter 8: The Compass and The Bloodline

Chapter 9: The Name She Never Knew

The coffee shop wasn't anything special. A little place tucked between old brick buildings, the kind of spot where the same folks sat in the same chairs every morning, reading the same newspapers and arguing about the same things they had been arguing about for twenty years. The kind of place that didn't care who you were when you walked in. It just gave you a cup of coffee and a quiet corner. And that's exactly what I needed. Because some conversations ain't meant to happen standing up. Some conversations need a table between two people. A little time. A little room for the world to stop spinning. I sat there waiting, hands wrapped around a coffee cup that had gone mostly untouched. Outside the window, Oakland moved along like it always did. Cars passing. People walking. Life happening. Funny thing about life... The world can keep moving even when yours feels like it stopped. Then the bell above the door rang. And there she was. Lauren. I watched her look around until she spotted me. She gave a small smile and walked over. She still had that same curious look in her eyes. That same way Bubba used to look when he was trying to figure something out. Like the world was a puzzle, and he was just waiting for somebody to hand him the missing pieces. She sat down across from me. "Hey, Greg." "Hey, kiddo." For a second, neither one of us said anything. Because what do you say when you're about to change somebody's entire understanding of where they came from? I took a slow breath. "Lauren... before I say anything, I want you to know something." She leaned forward slightly. "Okay..." "I didn't come here to bring you some sad story. I didn't come here to reopen some old wound. "No, kiddo. That's family." And then I told her about him. Not just the big things. Not just the war. Not just the tragedy. I told her about the real Bubba. The kid who could make anybody laugh. Chapter 10: The Daughter of Bubba Blue

Chapter 11: The Sacred Relay
Sleep never found me that night. Not even for a minute. I stayed out in the garage until dawn, sitting in my old lawn chair with Emerald Thunder parked a few feet away. The desert air was warm and still, carrying that familiar scent of sagebrush and baked asphalt. Every now and then, I’d catch myself staring at Thunder, my mind drifting back to that nineteen-sixty bill of sale. It's crazy, how one piece of paper can make a man question thirty years of memories. Some questions don’t leave you alone. They settle down somewhere deep inside, like a slow leak in a tire.
