 Continuity Map & Narrative Knowledge Guide [1].

From the Forrest Gump Universe,
The Legacy of Bubba Blue: The Life and Times of Big G-Mack McAllister 

This document is a detailed continuity map, as the story was specifically designed to be told in alignment with the narrative's order of events, the timing of discoveries, and the intended mystery structure of the story, without foreshadowing, or hinting at events which the audience has not heard yet. Preserve the experience of following Greg's life as it unfolds [1].

Important Narrative Rule [1].
This document contains both: 
1. Events that Greg experiences directly in the story [1].
2. Secret Historical information that exists in the complete story but is not known by Greg until later discoveries [1].

Hidden information must not be narrated before the story reaches the moment when Greg learns it [2]. The hosts should experience discoveries alongside Greg's narration, not ahead of it [2].

ERA 1: THE HIDDEN ORIGINS (1960–1964) [2].
Purpose of This Era [2].
This era contains the hidden history of Emerald Thunder before Greg becomes connected to the truck [2]. These events are important background information, but they are unknown to Greg until he expresses knowledge, and should remain hidden from the listener until the story reveals them naturally [3].

1960: The Original Purchase [3].
   Event Location: Palm Springs dealership [3].
   Characters: Bernard Blue, Roy Greene [3].
   Historical Truth: Bernard Blue purchases a brand-new 1960 Chevy K-10 [3]. The truck originally leaves the dealership painted Baby Blue [3].
   Why This Matters Later: This establishes Emerald Thunder's original connection to the Blue family and becomes the foundation of the final legacy reveal [3]. At this point in the story, however, the significance of this purchase is unknown to Greg [4].
   Knowledge Tracking:
       Greg's Knowledge: Emerald Thunder was simply a birthday gift from his father. Jerry is simply a man eager to buy the truck from Greg; 
       Listener's Intended Knowledge: Unknown; The listener should discover this information only when Greg discovers it [4].
       Hidden Information: Jerry informs Greg that his own father Roy Greene was the original owner of Emerald Thunder, which Greg believes until the scenes in 1991. 
       Reveal Point: Chapter 4, During the "truck sale/Adobe house" scenes, after Bubba's departure. 

Early 1960s: The Frame Job and The Hidden Truck [5].
   Event Locations: San Diego docks, Roy Greene's adobe home north of town. [5].
   Characters: Bernard Blue, Roy Greene, Jerry Greene [5].
   Historical Truth: Bernard Blue is falsely accused and imprisoned after being framed [5]. Roy Greene protects Emerald Thunder from being lost by hiding the truck and repainting it Emerald Green, concealing its original identity [5].
   Why This Matters Later: This explains why the truck's history appears mysterious and why its original identity was hidden for decades [5].
   Knowledge Tracking:
       Greg's Knowledge: Greg believes Bernard simply walked out on his family, and that the truck has no connection to the Blue family history [6].
       Listener's Intended Knowledge: The listener should not know the full truth until the story reveals it [6].
       Hidden Information: Roy Greene protected the truck; The truck was intentionally disguised; Jerry Greene knows parts of this history [6].
       Reveal Point: The information is revealed gradually, with the full truth coming during Jerry Greene's final explanation in 1991 [6].

ERA 2: THE GIFT AND THE BROTHERHOOD (1965–1966) [7].
1965: Harold McAllister Gives Greg Emerald Thunder [7].
   Event Location: McAllister home, Palm Springs [7].
   Characters: Greg McAllister (age fifteen), Harold McAllister [7].
   What Happens: Harold gives Greg the keys to Emerald Thunder [7]. That same night, Harold dies in a car accident [7].
   Greg's Understanding At The Time: Greg believes his father worked hard to buy him the truck as a surprise gift [7]. The truck becomes Greg's strongest physical connection to his father [7].
   Hidden Information: Roy Greene had helped arrange the truck's transfer to Harold as part of a larger promise involving Bernard Blue and the Blue family [7]. Greg does not know this [8].
   Emotional Importance: The truck begins as a symbol of love between father and son before becoming connected to friendship, loss, and legacy [8].

July 4th, 1966: The Birth of "Bubba" [8].
   Event Location: Lake Cahuilla [8].
   Characters: Greg, Benny Blue, Susie Anderson [8].
   What Happens: Greg refers to Benny as his "brother" [8]. Susie Anderson misunderstands and hears "Bubba" [8]. The nickname becomes Benny's lifelong identity [8].
   Why This Matters: A simple misunderstanding becomes one of the defining pieces of Benny's life [9]. This moment represents one of the story's central ideas: Small moments can shape an entire lifetime [9].

ERA 3: THE DEPARTURE AND THE SACRED RELAY (1967) [9].
Spring 1967: The Marking of Steel [9].
   Event Location: Greg's garage [9].
   Characters: Greg, Bubba [9].
   What Happens: Before leaving for Vietnam, Bubba carves his name into Emerald Thunder's passenger door [9].
   Immediate Meaning: At the time, it is a symbol of friendship and a way for Bubba to leave a piece of himself behind [10].
   Later Meaning: The carving becomes physical proof of their brotherhood and a lasting connection between generations [10].

Summer 1967: Greg Sells Emerald Thunder to Jerry Greene [10].
   Event Locations: Greg's garage, Jerry Greene's home [10].
   Characters: Greg, Jerry Greene [10].
   What Happens: Greg sells Emerald Thunder to Jerry [10]. Jerry pays more than Greg expected [10].
   Information Revealed At This Point: Greg learns Bernard Blue was wrongfully convicted, the truck's history is not what Greg believed, and the truck was originally Baby Blue [11].
   Information Still Hidden: Greg does not know the full connection between Bernard and the truck, Roy Greene's role, the Sacred Relay, or that Jerry's father will later be revealed as the man who saved him [11].

Late 1967: The Miracle Return [11].
   Event Locations: Highway 74, Roy Greene's property [11].
   Characters: Greg, Roy Greene [12].
   What Happens: After learning Bubba has died in Vietnam, Greg crashes during a storm [12]. A mysterious older man saves him [12]. Greg later discovers Emerald Thunder on the man's property and buys the truck back [12].
   Greg's Understanding At The Time: Greg believes a stranger happened to rescue him, the stranger happened to own his old truck, and the reunion is an incredible coincidence [12].
   Hidden Information: Greg does not know the man's identity is Roy Greene, that Roy knows exactly who Greg is, or that Roy is fulfilling a decades-old promise [12].
   Reveal Point: Chapter 11 — Jerry Greene reveals the identity of the "Desert Guardian Angel" [13].

ERA 4: THE LEGACY RESTORED (1991) [13].
Spring 1991: The Discovery of Roxy [13].
   Event Location: Indio / Bugsy's shop [13].
   Characters: Greg, Bugsy, Susie Anderson, Loren "Roxy" Azure [13].
   What Happens: Greg discovers that Bubba had a daughter [13]. He meets Roxy and realizes Bubba's legacy continued through her [13].
   Emotional Importance: Roxy becomes the connection between Greg's memories of Bubba and a future that still has meaning [13].

1991: The Bill of Sale Discovery [14].
   Event Location: Abandoned dealership [14].
   Characters: Greg, Roxy [14].
   What Happens: Greg and Roxy discover the original 1960 bill of sale [14]. The document lists Bernard Blue as the original owner of Emerald Thunder [14].
   Major Revelation: Greg learns the truck was never just his father's gift; it was part of a larger family legacy connecting the Blue and McAllister families [14].
   Information Still Unknown: Greg and Roxy do not yet fully understand Roy Greene's role [14]. They may initially question whether Roy acted dishonestly [15].

1991: The Final Truth — The Sacred Relay [15].
   Event Location: Roy Greene's adobe home [15].
   Characters: Greg, Roxy, Jerry Greene [15].
   What Happens: Jerry explains the hidden history [15]. Greg learns Roy Greene and Bernard Blue were lifelong brothers in spirit, Roy protected Emerald Thunder, Roy kept his promise to Bernard, and the truck was intentionally passed between families [15].
   Final Understanding: Emerald Thunder was never just a vehicle; it was a physical connection between two families, carrying the love, sacrifices, and promises of multiple generations [16].
   Final Revelation: The mysterious man who saved Greg in 1967 was Roy Greene [16]. The entire journey comes full circle [16].

Mandatory Revelation Barriers [16].
The following information must not be revealed before its designated point [16]:
   Before Chapter 10: Do not reveal Bernard Blue as original owner, or the full Blue family connection [16, 17].
   Before Chapter 11: Do not reveal Roy Greene as Greg's rescuer, Roy's relationship with Bernard, or the full Sacred Relay [17].

Final Adaptation Rule [17].
The story should always be experienced in the order Greg experiences it [17]. The audience may receive reflection and emotional meaning from older Greg's perspective, but hindsight must never reveal discoveries before their proper moment [17]. Preserve chronology, mystery, character knowledge, emotional pacing, and the importance of small moments [18]. The goal is not simply to tell what happened; it is to let the listener discover why it mattered [18].
