CHAPTER 7: The Storm of 1967 and the Miracle Anchor
But life can find a way to take something else from you—Something far more precious... And you might not even see it coming. One morning in the Fall of '67, I was heading out for a doctor’s appointment, snagging the mail and tossing it on the passenger seat. I had some time to kill, so I rifled through the envelopes. There it was—a letter from Miss Loretta, Bubba’s mama. I opened it. The words hit like a sucker punch: Bubba… my best friend… my brother in all but blood… killed in Vietnam. My stomach dropped, my chest tightened, panic bubbled. I read it again, hoping, praying, and then reality hit. Shock, grief, disbelief tangled inside me like desert vines. Could it really be true? The Biscayne didn’t wait for me to process it.

I slammed her into gear, raced down the street, rain pelting from a rare desert storm that made the mountains disappear in gray sheets. Wipers scrambled, memories of laughter, fights, long talks flashing like strobe lights. Grief hit, tidal and unrelenting. I didn't know where I was going. All I knew back then, is that my world was heavy, and I wanted to run from it. During that barrage of thoughts, something hit me... and I was instantly calm.
In a flash, the voice of pops was right there with me, in the cab of the Biscayne... It grabbed my entire world, and slowed it to a steady calm.
It told me my purpose. It gave me my reason...
Not to avenge the memory of what was lost... But to honor it and give devotion to it... to live for it... to breathe every breath for it...

But just as fast as that thought train pulled into the station, a curve in the road emerged through the thick sheet of raid in front of me. I tried slowing down before the curve, but it was too late... The Biscayne hydroplaned and fishtailed. I tried correcting but it was too late. The Biscayne had broken through a guardrail and launched off the side of the 111, and flipped multiple times before landing on its roof. The world flipped upside down. Darkness swallowed me. When I came to, it smelled of gasoline and I was bleeding from my forehead. I was dizzy, and my memory was a bit foggy. I knew I was in Indio, and I remember why I was driving so fast... But at first? The rest was a blur. Looking out the broken windsheild, I could see I was at the bottom of a steep embankment, in the brush. To the side, and up a small hill, sat a white little bungalow house, about 30 yards away. Suddenly, I noticed a frail old man was hovering above me. Somehow, he lifted me from the wreckage like some desert guardian angel. Wordlessly, he carried me to his truck and drove me to the hospital. My friend, "Bugsy" waited to take me home. I insisted we stop by the old man’s house to thank him. His truck sat in the yard, green like Emerald Thunder, an air freshener of a red-haired pinup girl dangling from the rearview mirror. He told me he got it last year from a guy in Indio. “Runs… barely. Interior’s seen better days, seat ripped, door panel marred by some kid’s carving.” “Marred door panel?” I raised an eyebrow. “Yeah,” he shrugged. “Looks like a kid carved a name in it.” My stomach dropped. “By chance… does it say...- 'Bubba'?” He nodded. I ran to the truck and peered inside. Bubba’s name, etched into the passenger door, staring back at me. Everything else the same. He offered it for five hundred. I handed over the cash. I lost Dad the day I got that truck. Now, the day my heart broke over Bubba, the truck returned, not as tragedy’s reminder but as a tiny, miraculous anchor. Emerald Thunder was back.

"That ain't just some old Chevy." I stepped out. "You know her?" He smiled. "Son..." He looked over the truck carefully. "I knew her before she was green." --- The old man explained that the dealership had been closed for years, but he had helped clean it out when ownership changed hands. A lot of things were left behind. Old advertisements. Customer records. Photographs. Paperwork nobody had bothered to throw away. "There's still some stuff in the back office," he said. "Nobody's touched it in years." We followed him inside. The building creaked around us. Dust covered everything. But underneath all of that... there were pieces of history still sitting exactly where people had left them. Old showroom photos. Sales records. Stacks of paperwork. A whole world that had been forgotten. Then the old man stopped. He pulled a folder from a cabinet. "I remember this one because I thought it was strange." He opened it. Inside was a copy of the original bill of sale. I leaned closer. Roxy stepped beside me. And together, we read it. The truck. The year. The dealership. The original buyer. Bernard Blue. I stared at the name. For a second, the room went quiet. Then I looked at Roxy. She wasn't looking at the truck anymore. She wasn't looking at the dealership. She was looking at that name. Because she knew it. "Bernard..." she whispered. I looked at her. "Yeah, kiddo." Her fingers touched the paper. "My grandfather." And right there, standing in an old dealership surrounded by forgotten pieces of yesterday, the past finally spoke clearly. This wasn't just about Emerald Thunder. This wasn't just about who owned an old Chevy pickup. This was about a man whose story had been buried. A man named Bernard Blue. Roxy's grandfather. Bubba's father. The man who brought home a brand-new blue pickup truck before his son ever touched the steering wheel. --- That night, back at the house, Roxy and I sat in the garage. The paperwork rested on the workbench. Emerald Thunder sat quietly behind us. Like she had been waiting all these years for somebody to finally ask the right questions. "I always thought I knew the whole story," I said. Roxy looked at me. "But you didn't." I shook my head. "No." I picked up the copy of the bill of sale. "Apparently, neither did Jerry." I looked at the name again. Bernard Blue. A name that had been buried for decades. A name that belonged to a family history nobody had finished telling. Jerry Greene. The man who bought Thunder from me. The man who had his own piece of the story. The man who said his dad Roy was the original owner. The only person who could have the answers we need. I looked out toward the desert night. "There's one person I need to talk to." Roxy waited. "Who?" I folded the paper. A lot of years had passed. A lot of miles had gone by. But some conversations... they don't disappear. They just wait. "Jerry Greene." And for the first time in a long time... I got that familiar feeling again... The feeling of a past, sitting out there somewhere, waiting to be found.
